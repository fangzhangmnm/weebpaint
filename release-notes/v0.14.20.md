## A smudge tool

The top bar now has a **Smudge** verb next to Pen, Eraser and Lasso. It comes in three flavours,
picked from a labelled dropdown in the context bar:

- **Finger (smear)** drags the paint that is already there.
- **Blend (dull)** softens and evens out what is under the brush. A **Dull** knob slides continuously
  between smearing and blending, so the two are really one tool with a dial.
- **Wet finger (paint)** carries your current colour into the stroke, with **Dilution** and **Memory**
  controls for how much colour it brings and how long it remembers what it picked up.

Mixing happens in your choice of colour space — **Plain mix**, **Vivid mix** (keeps saturation) or
**Pigment mix** (spectral, closer to real paint). Smudge has its own strength dial, its own brush rack,
and its default is a soft 32 px brush at half strength. Its settings, including dilution and memory,
persist with the app.

The **Blur** and **Sharpen** brushes were tuned in the same round: their dab spacing now follows the
brush like every other brush (it had been stuck at a hard-coded value since an old build), with a
floor of 10 % so a blur stroke stays smooth.

## Smudge, blur, sharpen and liquify now run on the GPU

All four were CPU tools. They now run as GPU region programs, translated dab for dab from the CPU
originals and checked against them pixel by pixel. The visible change is in **Liquify**: the stall on
every pen-down that testers reported is gone — the old engine copied the whole content box and
allocated a full-size buffer at the start of each stroke; the new one grows a displacement field only
as far as the stroke reaches.

Shader compilation for these programs happens in idle time after your document is already on screen,
never as a wait at startup. Devices whose WebGL cannot provide floating-point render targets get a
message instead of a broken tool.

## Pressure

- A **Pressure sensitivity** setting in the menu with four levels, for pens that report too much or
  too little.
- Each brush now has a **Pressure curve** — the same keyframe curve editor as the Curves filter, with
  draggable handles and weighted tangents.
- Holding the pen still at the start of a stroke no longer builds up into an ink blot.

## Curves and Gradient map

The **Curves** filter was rebuilt on a keyframe curve editor: drag handles, add and delete points with
real buttons, choose tangent modes per point, and pull handles to different lengths on each side
(weighted tangents, as in Unity or Blender). The window can be dragged larger from any edge.

A new **Gradient map** filter maps luminance through an editable colour ramp; colour stops connect to
the current colour target, so you edit a stop with the same picker you use everywhere else.

## Top bar: verbs, with the details in the context bar

The top bar is now a row of verbs — Pen, Eraser, Smudge, Lasso, Shape brush — and each verb's sub-tools
(which kind of pen, which kind of selection, which kind of smudge) live in the left segment of the
context bar below it. Long-press a verb to call that bar up. Rows of unlabelled icons were replaced with
labelled dropdowns.

The eyedropper moved: there is now a one-shot **pick** button in the left column between the two
sliders (tap for one sample, hold to keep sampling), plus an eyedropper on the palette. After a pick you
are returned to the tool you were using. The old top-bar eyedropper is gone.

Smaller things in the same pass: the layer adjustments drawer became a popup that closes itself when
you switch layers; the blend-mode dropdown shows its text again; the reference window has a close
button; the main-menu button toggles the menu; dropdowns are sized to their own widest entry.

## Shape brush is back as its own button

The **Shape brush (S)** returns as a separate top-bar verb with its own context bar, behaving as it did
before the 0.14 experiments. The vanishing-point editor got a ✓ to return to the shape brush, the grid
stepper is more compact, and one-point perspective no longer swaps rows and columns.

## Selection belongs to the document, not the tool

Switching from Lasso to the fill bucket carries your current selection into fill automatically.
Switching back drops the fill preview but keeps the selection.

## Gallery and sync

- If the gallery gets stuck, it now offers **Diagnostic log** and **Reload app** on the spot.
- A hung IndexedDB no longer takes the gallery down with it; the app detects the hang and recovers.
- When your cloud sign-in expires overnight, the gallery keeps showing the full cloud list rather than
  silently dropping to local files only. Only an explicit sign-out switches you to local-only.
- Closing the tab mid-read no longer leaves a lock behind.
- The update notice and the reload after an update wait for sign-in to finish (up to 8 s), so a
  reload no longer lands in the middle of a Microsoft login round-trip.
- The diagnostic log can be copied reliably again, and can be shared as a `.txt` file.

## Also

Eight new icons (blend, blur, sharpen, liquify, chevrons, 1:1, help) replace the placeholder glyphs
that had been standing in for them.
